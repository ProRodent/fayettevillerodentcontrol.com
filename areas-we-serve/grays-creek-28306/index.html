Gray's Creek Rodent Pressure and Treatment

Gray's Creek sits in the southwestern corner of Cumberland County in the 28306 ZIP: rural farmland, wooded creek corridors, and widely spaced residential properties on large lots. The rodent pressure here's fundamentally different from urban Fayetteville. Norway rats are present at higher density in agricultural drainage ditches and barn areas. Field mice and deer mice are common alongside house mice. The outdoor source populations are genuinely large: sustained by agricultural food sources, dense vegetation, and earthen drainage banks that are never disturbed.

📋 Real Scenario

A homeowner near the Cape Fear River corridor called us after finding Norway rat burrows along their foundation. The river-adjacent yard plus a backyard compost setup had created ideal habitat. We treated the burrows, installed perimeter exclusion at the foundation grade, and recommended compost relocation. The follow-up inspection found no new burrow activity in the treated zone.

Properties in Gray's Creek span every common housing type, from older farmhouses with crawl spaces that have significant foundation gap exposure, to newer rural residential construction with slab foundations and attached garages. The common thread is the agricultural-adjacent boundary: no matter how tight the construction, properties within 100-200 feet of working fields, grain storage, or barn areas see sustained rodent population pressure that interior treatment alone can't resolve.

Exclusion sealing is the most important investment for Gray's Creek property owners experiencing rodent issues, because the outdoor source population can't be eliminated, permanently blocking structural entry is the only approach that provides lasting results. We advise honestly on which entry points are highest priority for a rural property's specific configuration before any sealing work begins.
