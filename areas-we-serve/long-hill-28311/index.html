Where rodents get in

Entry Points We Check in Long Hill

NC State says gaps of about a quarter inch let mice in and a half inch lets adult rats in. At a property in Long Hill, the inspection covers the garage and exterior door clearance, pipe and wire entries through walls and the crawl space and attic vents.

UC IPM notes that Norway rats dig burrows along foundations and beneath rubbish and woodpiles, while roof rats nest high in attics and wall voids. For a home in Long Hill, both get checked: the ground along the foundation and the roofline.

Checklist

What to Check on a Property in Long Hill

  • Door bottoms and garage door seals: NC State advises keeping the clearance at the floor to a quarter inch or less, with kick plates or rodent-proof sweeps where doors take wear.
  • Foundation edges: look for burrows beside the wall and under steps, sheds and woodpiles, which are Norway rat signs.
  • Attic: look for droppings, rub marks and shredded insulation. UC IPM says roof rats nest above ground in attics and wall voids.
  • Sheds, fences and outbuildings: each is a separate route in and gets the same sealing as the house.
  • Pipe, wire and dryer vent entries: UC IPM says gaps wider than a quarter inch need metal or concrete, with steel wool only as a temporary plug.
