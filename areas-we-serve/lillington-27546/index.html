Where rodents get in

Entry Points We Check in Lillington

UC IPM notes that Norway rats dig burrows along foundations and beneath rubbish and woodpiles, while roof rats nest high in attics and wall voids. For a home in Lillington, both get checked: the ground along the foundation and the roofline.

Droppings show how active a problem is. NC State puts a rat at 30 to 50 droppings a day and a mouse at 50 to 75, so fresh droppings in the attic, garage or under a sink in Lillington are the cue to call.

Checklist

What to Check on a Property in Lillington

  • Attic and crawl space vents: screen them with quarter-inch hardware cloth. Foam alone and chicken wire do not hold.
  • Rooflines and trees: UC IPM lists trimming limbs back from the roof as a way to cut the route roof rats use.
  • Trash and pet food: keep both in sealed containers off the ground, since food outside keeps a rat population close to the house.
  • Door bottoms and garage door seals: NC State advises keeping the clearance at the floor to a quarter inch or less, with kick plates or rodent-proof sweeps where doors take wear.
  • Foundation edges: look for burrows beside the wall and under steps, sheds and woodpiles, which are Norway rat signs.
