Rodent Control Across the Sandhills: How Cumberland, Harnett, Hoke, and Robeson Counties Differ

Published May 1, 2026

Rodent control follows the same rules across the Sandhills, but the places differ. This guide walks through the nearby counties using published facts about each town and shows what to check on a property in each.

Key Takeaways

  • The sealing and sanitation rules are the same in every county
  • Water edges, interstates and older buildings are the local details that change what gets checked first
  • Every town page on this site lists its sources

What the Region Has in Common

The Sandhills is a 10 to 35 mile wide region along the inland edge of the Atlantic Coastal Plain with very sandy soils and pine-dominated vegetation. Cumberland County has a land area of 652.56 square miles and had 334,728 residents at the 2020 census, with Fayetteville as the county seat. It borders Harnett, Sampson, Bladen, Robeson, Hoke and Moore counties, per Wikipedia.

How small a gap a mouse needs

The rules for closing gaps do not change from county to county. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Vents need metal, not filler

Vent screening is the same job on any foundation. NC State Extension recommends quarter inch hardware cloth over vents and does not recommend expanding foam as a stand-alone seal. Rodents chew soft materials, so metal mesh is what keeps an opening closed. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Cumberland County

Cumberland County includes Fayetteville, Hope Mills, Spring Lake, Eastover, Stedman and Fort Bragg. Cross Creek flows through Fayetteville to the Cape Fear River, and the Little River runs near Spring Lake. For homes near these waters, UC IPM's note that Norway rats burrow along foundations and beneath debris is the reason the foundation edge is checked first.

Where rats nest

Check burrows near water edges, outbuildings and woodpiles. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Harnett County

Dunn sits on US 301 and US 421 with I-95 exits 72 and 73 on its east side, and had 9,263 residents in 2010. Angier is on NC 55 and NC 210. Erwin is on US 421 with the Cape Fear River on part of its western border and Juniper Creek nearby. Lillington is a Cape Fear River town.

Near an interstate exit, commercial food waste and dumpsters sit close to homes, so dumpster areas next to a property are part of the inspection. Near the river, foundation edges and outbuildings come first.

Hoke and Robeson Counties

Raeford, in Hoke County, is on US 401 with Rockfish Creek on its northeast border and had 4,611 residents in 2010. Lumberton, in Robeson County, is on I-95 and I-74 with the Lumber River through downtown, and had 21,542 residents in 2010. Pembroke is home to UNC Pembroke, and Red Springs, Maxton, Fairmont, Rowland and Saint Pauls are smaller Robeson County towns.

Storms and flooding push rodents indoors

Lumberton and other river towns have been flooded in recent storms, so that guidance applies there. NC State Extension notes that flooding pushes rodents into attics and that Norway rats are the most common rat in North Carolina. After a storm, new gaps and wet debris are both worth checking. (Source: NC State Extension, pest problems after storms: rodents)

Older mill buildings in Red Springs and Saint Pauls give a sense of the region's history. Empty mills and other vacant buildings give rodents cover, and a vacant structure next to a home is worth reporting to its owner.

Moore County

Pinehurst (13,124 residents in 2010) sits on US 15 and US 501 with creeks nearby, and Southern Pines and Aberdeen are its neighbors. Pinehurst's village center is on the National Register. Older and historic buildings call for sealing that fits the building, with metal mesh and flashing in place of anything that has to be cut into trim.

Screening vents against rats

Original vents on older buildings often need new mesh. UC IPM recommends half inch mesh over vents and says chicken wire is not enough to stop rats. NC State's quarter inch hardware cloth is the tighter standard, and it also keeps mice out. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

A Checklist for Any Property in the Region

  • Door bottoms and garage door seals: keep the clearance to a quarter inch or less
  • Attic and crawl space vents: quarter inch hardware cloth, not foam
  • Pipe, wire and dryer vent entries: metal or concrete for gaps over a quarter inch
  • Foundation edge: burrows, gnawing and washed soil
  • Trash and pet food: sealed containers, off the ground

More From the Published Guidance

Why a dead rodent can end up in a wall

For Rodent Control Across the Sandhills: How Cumberland, Harnett, Hoke, and Robeson Counties Differ: The EPA says anticoagulant baits take about 4 days to 2 weeks to kill. A poisoned rodent can die inside a wall, attic or crawl space, which is why trapping is preferred indoors. Delayed action is the main reason indoor bait causes odor problems. (Source: US EPA, rodenticides)

What mouse droppings tell you

NC State Extension puts a mouse at roughly 50 to 75 droppings a day, each about a quarter to a half inch long. A small area can hold a lot of them, which is why a mouse problem is often found by droppings first. That volume is why even a small mouse problem leaves obvious evidence. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

How fast mice multiply

For Rodent Control Across the Sandhills: How Cumberland, Harnett, Hoke, and Robeson Counties Differ: UC IPM reports that a female house mouse can have 5 to 10 litters a year of about 5 to 6 pups, with gestation of 18 to 21 days and sexual maturity at 5 to 6 weeks. A few mice can become dozens in a season. Waiting a few weeks can multiply the number of animals to deal with. (Source: UC IPM, house mouse)

Door gaps and sweeps

NC State Extension advises keeping the clearance under a door to no more than a quarter inch and mentions kick plates and rodent-proof door sweeps for garage and storage doors that take wear. A strip of daylight under a door is a gap that a rodent can use. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Tamper-resistant stations

For Rodent Control Across the Sandhills: How Cumberland, Harnett, Hoke, and Robeson Counties Differ: The EPA requires tamper-resistant bait stations where children under six, pets or wildlife could reach the bait. A station that a child or a dog can open is not an acceptable placement. Placement matters as much as the product. (Source: US EPA, rodenticide product restrictions)

Where rat traps go

For Rodent Control Across the Sandhills: How Cumberland, Harnett, Hoke, and Robeson Counties Differ: UC IPM says to space snap traps about 10 to 20 feet apart for rats and to set them off the ground, on beams or shelves, for roof rats, which travel along elevated routes. Spacing and height both change how often a trap is found by the animal. (Source: UC IPM, rats)
