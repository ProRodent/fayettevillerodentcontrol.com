About this service

What Attic Rodent Proofing Covers

Attics are warm, dry and full of insulation, which makes them attractive to roof rats and mice. They get in through gable vents, soffits, roof edges, ridge vents, plumbing stacks and gaps where roof meets wall.

Attic proofing looks at the openings from both sides, outside at the roof and inside with a light. Each gap is closed with metal or mesh, and vents are covered with hardware cloth. Trapping and cleanup are scheduled around the sealing so animals are not shut in.

Signs

When to Call About Attic Rodent Proofing

  • Scratching in the attic at night
  • Droppings on insulation
  • Chewed vent screens or soffits
  • Stains on ceilings
  • Smell from the attic

A common mistake

Sealing the attic before the animals are out is the usual error. The animals stay trapped, die in the structure and cause odor. The fix is trapping first, then sealing.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Attic Rodent Proofing

Where rats nest

Roof rats nest in attics, so attic work follows the roof rat route. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Screening vents against rats

Attic vent screening has to be strong enough to stop rats. UC IPM recommends half inch mesh over vents and says chicken wire is not enough to stop rats. NC State's quarter inch hardware cloth is the tighter standard, and it also keeps mice out. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

What rat droppings tell you

Droppings in the attic tell how active the problem is. NC State Extension puts a rat at roughly 30 to 50 droppings a day, each about a half to three quarters of an inch long. Fresh, dark droppings in a spot mean the route is in use now. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

How small a gap a mouse needs

Mice need only a quarter inch to reach an attic. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Cutting the route to the roof

Limb contact is checked at every attic job. UC IPM lists limb trimming, with a clearance range of 3 to 6 feet, as a way to cut off the route roof rats use to reach a roof. Trees and wires that touch the house act as bridges. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Attic Rodent Proofing: Questions People Ask

How do rodents get into an attic?

Through gable and ridge vents, soffits, roof edges, plumbing stacks and gaps where wires enter. Roof rats often use limbs and wires to reach the roof.

Will sealing trap animals inside?

Not if it is planned. Sealing is timed with trapping so the last openings are closed once activity has stopped.

What about insulation?

Droppings and nesting damage insulation. After cleanup it may need to be replaced, and that is covered on its own page.
