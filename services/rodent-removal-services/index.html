What We Remove

Rodent Species in Fayetteville

Species 01

Norway Rat

Heavy-bodied ground burrower. Concentrated along Cape Fear corridor, drainage easements, and sandy-soil foundations. Enters at ground level. Trapping plus burrow treatment plus foundation exclusion.

Species 02

Roof Rat

Slender agile climber. Uses longleaf pine canopy in Haymount and Massey Hill to reach eave gaps. Enters attics, wall cavities, and upper-story voids. Canopy break plus attic trapping plus roofline exclusion.

Species 03

House Mouse

Small, prolific, year-round breeder in Fayetteville's subtropical climate. Enters through pencil-width gaps. FSU-area rentals, Fort Liberty housing, and Sandhills crawl-space homes are high-pressure areas.

FAQ

Rodent Removal Questions: Fayetteville

What happens during a rodent removal inspection in Fayetteville?

We walk the full property: exterior foundation, crawl space, attic, interior living areas, and utility spaces. We map droppings, identify species from physical evidence, locate entry points, and assess infestation severity. You receive a written plan before any treatment begins.

Can you remove rodents the same day you inspect?

Yes. For confirmed active infestations, we carry trap and bait station equipment on every service vehicle. Same-day inspection and same-day treatment initiation is available across most of Cumberland County. Call (844) 635-0403 to confirm availability for your address.

What rodents do you remove besides rats and mice?

Our removal service covers Norway rats, roof rats, and house mice, the three species common to Fayetteville and the Sandhills. We don't provide wildlife removal services for squirrels, raccoons, or other non-rodent animals.

How long does a rodent removal program take?

Light infestations in a sealed property can clear in 1–2 weeks of active trapping. Moderate infestations typically require 3–4 weeks and multiple visits. Heavy or multi-species infestations, especially in larger properties with multiple entry points, can require 6–8 weeks. Exclusion sealing is scheduled after population decline is confirmed.
