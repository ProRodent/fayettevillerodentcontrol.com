About this service

What Attic Cleanup and Sanitization Covers

An attic holds heat and dust, so rodent waste dries and spreads easily. Insulation absorbs urine and holds odor, and droppings can be scattered across a large area. Cleaning has to be planned to avoid stirring up that dust.

The work follows the CDC order: air out, protect, wet with disinfectant, wipe or remove, bag and disinfect. Soiled insulation is removed in bags, not dragged across the attic, and the attic is checked for entry points before new insulation is considered.

Signs

When to Call About Attic Cleanup and Sanitization

  • Flattened or stained insulation
  • Droppings scattered across joists
  • Strong odor in the attic
  • Chewed wiring

A common mistake

Cleaning before the rodents are gone is the main mistake. They contaminate the area again. Another is dragging soiled insulation through the house.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Attic Cleanup and Sanitization

Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings

Dry attic dust is the main hazard during cleanup. The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests, because that puts dust into the air. Wet them with disinfectant first and wipe them up. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

Cleanup steps from the CDC

Order of steps matters in an attic. The CDC advises airing out the space for 30 minutes, wearing gloves, soaking droppings and nests with disinfectant, wiping them up with paper towels, double bagging the waste and washing hands afterward. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

What rat droppings tell you

Rat droppings can be widely scattered. NC State Extension puts a rat at roughly 30 to 50 droppings a day, each about a half to three quarters of an inch long. Fresh, dark droppings in a spot mean the route is in use now. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Where rats nest

Nest location is checked before cleanup begins. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

What works to seal gaps

Sealing is confirmed before cleanup. UC IPM says gaps wider than a quarter inch should be closed with metal or concrete. Steel wool is a temporary plug, and it lists plastic, rubber, vinyl, expanding foam and wood as materials that do not hold against mice. (Source: UC IPM, house mouse)

Prevention is the main control

For Attic Cleanup and Sanitization: The CDC states that controlling rodents in and around the home is the primary way to prevent rodent-borne disease such as hantavirus. Sealing and sanitation come before any trap or bait. Prevention work, not cleanup alone, is the main control. (Source: CDC, rodent control)

How small a gap a mouse needs

For Attic Cleanup and Sanitization: NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. Small gaps are easy to miss without measuring. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Why clean an attic after rodents?

Waste and nesting material can carry disease and odor. The CDC says controlling rodents and cleaning safely are the main ways to prevent exposure.

Do I need to replace insulation?

If it is soiled or nested in, usually yes. See the insulation replacement page.
