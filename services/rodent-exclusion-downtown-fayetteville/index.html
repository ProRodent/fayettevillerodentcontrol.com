Why Exclusion Matters Most Downtown

Downtown Fayetteville's historic building stock shares specific deterioration patterns that drive exclusion-focused treatment. The brick foundations have original lime-mortar joints that have lost integrity after 80-140 years; the galvanized vent infrastructure has corroded through its mounting frames; and the utility penetrations that brought modern plumbing and electrical service into 1900s buildings were sealed with materials that have long since deteriorated.

The mid-block construction layout makes downtown rodent issues particularly persistent. Adjacent buildings share continuous foundation walls, common alley access, and overlapping drainage corridors that let Norway rat populations extend across multiple properties before being noticed at any one address. Sealing only the visible activity points without addressing the structural pathways between buildings produces temporary suppression but not lasting results.

FAQ

Downtown Fayetteville Exclusion FAQ

Will exclusion work damage my downtown historic building?

No. We use preservation-compatible materials and methods specifically because downtown's housing stock has irreplaceable historic value. Visible repairs use period-appropriate materials; structural repairs prioritize effectiveness without compromising character.

Why is exclusion more important downtown than in newer neighborhoods?

Because the mid-block continuous foundation construction allows rodent populations to extend across multiple properties. Bait-only treatment in this environment achieves temporary suppression but no lasting result, exclusion changes accessibility.

How long does typical downtown exclusion work take?

Inspection plus initial sealing takes one day. Follow-up to verify no continued activity and to address any additional discovered entry points takes a second visit 2-3 weeks later.

Do you coordinate with adjacent building owners?

When the rodent issue clearly extends across properties, yes. Coordinated treatment with adjacent owners (with permission) addresses the cross-property population more effectively than single-property treatment. We facilitate that conversation.

What's the typical cost premium for downtown exclusion vs suburban?

About 25-40% higher than standard residential exclusion scope, reflecting the preservation-grade materials, longer labor time per entry point, and the more complex inspection of older construction.
