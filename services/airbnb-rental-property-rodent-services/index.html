About this service

What Rental Property Rodent Services Covers

Rental properties are often empty for stretches, and food left by guests can be enough for mice. Owners who live elsewhere may not see the first signs, so a small problem can grow before anyone notices.

The service combines inspection, sealing and cleanup. We check the property for signs, close the gaps that let rodents in, set traps and clean droppings so the unit is ready for the next guest or tenant. We recommend simple routines such as sealed trash and no open food storage.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Rental Property Rodent Services

How fast mice multiply

Empty periods give mice time to multiply. UC IPM reports that a female house mouse can have 5 to 10 litters a year of about 5 to 6 pups, with gestation of 18 to 21 days and sexual maturity at 5 to 6 weeks. A few mice can become dozens in a season. (Source: UC IPM, house mouse)

What mouse droppings tell you

Droppings are the first sign owners find. NC State Extension puts a mouse at roughly 50 to 75 droppings a day, each about a quarter to a half inch long. A small area can hold a lot of them, which is why a mouse problem is often found by droppings first. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Cleanup steps from the CDC

Cleanup is done before the next stay. The CDC advises airing out the space for 30 minutes, wearing gloves, soaking droppings and nests with disinfectant, wiping them up with paper towels, double bagging the waste and washing hands afterward. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

Checking traps daily

Trap checks are scheduled between stays. NC State Extension advises checking traps daily and keeping mouse traps for mice and rat traps for rats. A trap that is too small for the animal can injure it without killing it and teach it to avoid the spot. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

How small a gap a mouse needs

Mice need only a quarter inch. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

What the EPA allows consumers

For Rental Property Rodent Services: The EPA restricts consumer rodenticides to ready-to-use bait stations and says second-generation anticoagulant products are not for consumer use. Anything stronger is for trained applicators. These rules shape what can and cannot be used at a home. (Source: US EPA, rodenticides)

Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings

For Rental Property Rodent Services: The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests, because that puts dust into the air. Wet them with disinfectant first and wipe them up. Dust is the hazard, so wetting first is the key step. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)
