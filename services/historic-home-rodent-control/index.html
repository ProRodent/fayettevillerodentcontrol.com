About this service

What Historic Home Rodent Control Covers

Older houses often have lap siding, decorative trim, brick foundations with settled mortar, original vents and long-open crawl spaces. Each of those details can hide a gap, and each needs a repair that does not damage the building.

Work on a historic home is done conservatively. The inspection records original features, and sealing uses metal mesh and flashing that can be fitted without cutting into trim wherever possible. Trapping and cleanup follow the same steps as any other house.

Signs

When to Call About Historic Home Rodent Control

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Historic Home Rodent Control

Vents need metal, not filler

Original vents often have damaged screens. NC State Extension recommends quarter inch hardware cloth over vents and does not recommend expanding foam as a stand-alone seal. Rodents chew soft materials, so metal mesh is what keeps an opening closed. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Where rats nest

Roof rats and Norway rats both use older homes. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

What works to seal gaps

Metal and concrete repair what foam cannot. UC IPM says gaps wider than a quarter inch should be closed with metal or concrete. Steel wool is a temporary plug, and it lists plastic, rubber, vinyl, expanding foam and wood as materials that do not hold against mice. (Source: UC IPM, house mouse)

Checking traps daily

Trap checks continue until catches stop. NC State Extension advises checking traps daily and keeping mouse traps for mice and rat traps for rats. A trap that is too small for the animal can injure it without killing it and teach it to avoid the spot. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Cleanup steps from the CDC

Cleanup uses safe handling steps. The CDC advises airing out the space for 30 minutes, wearing gloves, soaking droppings and nests with disinfectant, wiping them up with paper towels, double bagging the waste and washing hands afterward. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

What mouse droppings tell you

For Historic Home Rodent Control: NC State Extension puts a mouse at roughly 50 to 75 droppings a day, each about a quarter to a half inch long. A small area can hold a lot of them, which is why a mouse problem is often found by droppings first. That volume is why even a small mouse problem leaves obvious evidence. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)
