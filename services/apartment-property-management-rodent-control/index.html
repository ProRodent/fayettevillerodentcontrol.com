About this service

What Apartment and Property Management Rodent Control Covers

In a multifamily building, rodents do not stay in one unit. They use shared walls, pipe chases, ceilings and trash areas to move, so treating a single apartment often moves the problem next door instead of ending it.

The approach is building-wide: inspect the reported unit and the units around it, map where activity runs, seal the chases and penetrations that connect them and agree on trash and storage practices. Property managers get a written summary of findings.

When to Call About Apartment and Property Management Rodent Control

A common mistake

Treating only the complaining unit is the most common mistake. Rodents move through shared walls and chases, so neighbors need to be checked.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Apartment and Property Management Rodent Control

How fast mice multiply

Mice breed quickly, which is why a single unit is rarely the whole problem. UC IPM reports that a female house mouse can have 5 to 10 litters a year of about 5 to 6 pups, with gestation of 18 to 21 days and sexual maturity at 5 to 6 weeks. A few mice can become dozens in a season. (Source: UC IPM, house mouse)

How small a gap a mouse needs

Pipe gaps behind sinks are usually bigger than a quarter inch. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

What mouse droppings tell you

Droppings help map the route. NC State Extension puts a mouse at roughly 50 to 75 droppings a day, each about a quarter to a half inch long. A small area can hold a lot of them, which is why a mouse problem is often found by droppings first. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Checking traps daily

Trap checks are scheduled across the building. NC State Extension advises checking traps daily and keeping mouse traps for mice and rat traps for rats. A trap that is too small for the animal can injure it without killing it and teach it to avoid the spot. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

What works to seal gaps

Sealing between units uses metal, not foam. UC IPM says gaps wider than a quarter inch should be closed with metal or concrete. Steel wool is a temporary plug, and it lists plastic, rubber, vinyl, expanding foam and wood as materials that do not hold against mice. (Source: UC IPM, house mouse)

Screening vents against rats

For Apartment and Property Management Rodent Control: UC IPM recommends half inch mesh over vents and says chicken wire is not enough to stop rats. NC State's quarter inch hardware cloth is the tighter standard, and it also keeps mice out. Mesh size is a small detail that decides whether a vent stays closed. (Source: UC IPM, rats)
