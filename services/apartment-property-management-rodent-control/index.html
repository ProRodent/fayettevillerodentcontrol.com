The Multi-Unit Problem

Why Treating One Unit Isn't Enough

As the National Pest Management Association notes, multi-unit rodent control fails when only the reported unit is treated, our apartment managers protocol covers the full approach —. Rats and mice move through shared wall voids, plumbing chases, HVAC ducts, and crawl space areas that connect units horizontally and vertically. Treating unit 4B while 4A and 5B are uninspected isn't a treatment — it's a pressure valve that redirects the population temporarily and generates a callback in 30 days.

🔧 Technician Insight

Multi-unit infestations rarely stay in one unit. Rats and mice travel through shared wall voids, utility chases, and HVAC plenums. Treating only the complaining tenant's unit guarantees the problem reappears in the adjacent unit within 30 days. We assess affected and adjacent units together.

Effective multi-unit treatment requires assessing both the reported unit and its adjacent neighbors simultaneously. We inspect all units sharing a wall, floor, or ceiling with the reported infestation, identify which units show active signs versus buffer exposure, and treat the full affected zone in a coordinated sequence. Population reduction in the core units happens before exclusion sealing, sealing too early traps rodents inside walls and creates dead-animal odor problems.

Fort Liberty-adjacent rental markets in Spring Lake, Kings Grant, and Long Hill present a specific challenge: PCS-cycle turnovers leave units briefly vacant between tenants, and a vacant unit with unsecured utility penetrations is the fastest path for rodents to establish a foothold that spills into occupied neighboring units. We recommend turnover inspections as part of any property management program covering 10 or more units near Fort Liberty.

FAQ

Common Questions

Why do multi-unit buildings need coordinated treatment?

Treating only the reported unit drives rodents into adjacent units through shared wall cavities, utility chases, and plumbing voids. Effective multi-unit treatment requires assessing and treating affected and buffer units simultaneously to prevent population migration.

Can you coordinate with tenants directly?

Yes, if the property manager authorizes it. We can contact tenants directly to schedule access, provide pre-treatment preparation instructions, and follow up after service. Alternatively, we work entirely through the property manager.

Do you provide documentation for landlord-tenant disputes?

Our service reports document inspection date, units assessed, findings per unit, treatment applied, and follow-up schedule, useful for demonstrating landlord compliance with North Carolina habitability standards.

How do you handle a tenant who refuses access?

We document the refusal and the PM-authorized access attempt in writing. We treat all accessible units and note which were refused. NC landlord-tenant law governs tenant access obligations, we recommend consulting legal counsel if a refusal is blocking remediation.
