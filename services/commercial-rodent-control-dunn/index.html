Dunn Commercial Treatment Categories

Dunn's commercial activity centers on three categories: the I-95 interchange commercial (truck stops, fuel, fast-food, hotels at Exit 73), the downtown mill-era commercial blocks (older brick storefronts, restaurants, small retail), and the US-301 / NC-55 corridor commercial running through town. Each category has its own rodent-pressure profile and treatment approach. The I-95 interchange properties see commercial-corridor pressure similar to Lumberton's I-95 nodes. The mill-era downtown shares the foundation-condition issues of other pre-1950s NC commercial districts. The corridor strips fall in between.

🔧 Technician Insight

Health-inspection citations for rodent evidence usually trace back to one of three failures: dock door seal gaps, dumpster proximity to entry doors, or interior bait stations placed without a written IPM plan. Each is preventable with the right service schedule and documentation.

Mill-era downtown construction in Dunn dates predominantly to the textile and tobacco industry era of the late 1800s through the 1930s. The brick foundations, original mortar work, and continuous-block construction patterns are similar to what we see in Lumberton, Aberdeen, and other NC rail-era county-adjacent towns. Treatment scope on these properties leans heavily on structural exclusion at foundation level rather than baiting alone.
