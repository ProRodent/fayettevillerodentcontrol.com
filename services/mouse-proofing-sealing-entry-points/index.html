The Fine-Gap Problem

Mice Need Only a Quarter Inch

A house mouse can compress through a gap the width of a pencil, roughly one quarter of an inch. That's smaller than the gap under most interior doors, smaller than a worn garage door bottom seal, smaller than a dryer vent transition joint that has separated by a few millimeters. Mouse proofing is fundamentally different from rat proofing in its granularity: rat-exclusion-level work misses mouse entry points routinely, because the gaps that admit mice are too small to register as significant in a rat-focused survey.

🔧 Technician Insight

Mouse-proofing is incremental, you seal in order of likelihood. Garage perimeter and utility chases first (60% of mouse entries). Then dryer vents, AC line penetrations, and pipe-wall gaps. Last: trim-board gaps and door sweeps. Doing it in this order means most homes solve the problem before getting to the lower-priority gaps.

We conduct mouse proofing surveys with fine-gap identification as the explicit objective: flashlight inspection of utility penetrations at every wall they pass through (pipes, conduit, cables), garage door bottom seal condition check across the full width of the door, dryer vent transition and exterior cap inspection, door sweep and threshold gap measurement at every exterior door, and foundation crack survey at the mud-sill level. These are the seven categories that generate 90% of mouse entry in Cumberland County residential properties, and most of them require specific tools and angles to identify from the ground.

Sealing materials for mouse-width gaps differ from rat exclusion: copper mesh packed into utility gaps (similar techniques apply to crawl space exclusion in Sandhills soil) (foam alone is insufficient), door sweep replacement with full-contact seals, garage door bottom seal replacement, and dryer vent bird-screen installation. None of these are dramatic interventions. they're the maintenance items that were deferred and added up to an infestation.

FAQ

Common Questions

What's the smallest gap that admits a house mouse?

House mice can compress through gaps as small as 1/4 inch (roughly the diameter of a pencil). This is smaller than most worn garage door seals, door sweep gaps, and utility penetration gaps that are never specifically sealed.

How's mouse proofing different from rat exclusion?

Mouse proofing focuses on finer gaps than rat exclusion typically addresses. A rat exclusion survey looks for gaps of 1/2 inch and larger. A mouse proofing survey targets 1/4-inch gaps, which requires closer inspection of utility penetrations, door seals, and foundation cracks.

Can you replace garage door seals during the same visit?

We assess and document garage door bottom seal condition during the survey. Replacement is quoted separately and can usually be completed during the same visit for standard residential garage doors.
