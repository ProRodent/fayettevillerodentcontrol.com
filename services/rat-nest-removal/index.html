About this service

What Rat Nest Removal Covers

Rat nests are built from shredded insulation, paper, fabric and other soft material. Roof rats build them in attics, wall voids and other elevated spots, while Norway rats dig burrows beside foundations and under debris.

Removing the nest means locating it, treating the animals first and then clearing nesting material with gloves, disinfectant and double bags. The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry nests or droppings because the dust can carry disease.

Signs

When to Call About Rat Nest Removal

  • Shredded insulation or paper in a pile
  • Droppings clustered near a pile of material
  • A musty smell near the attic or wall
  • Rub marks leading to a corner
  • Burrow holes with soft soil

A common mistake

Removing a nest without removing the animals is the usual mistake. They just rebuild. Removing it without gloves or wet cleaning is the other risk.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Rat Nest Removal

Where rats nest

Species decides where the nest is. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings

Dry nests should not be disturbed with a broom or vacuum. The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests, because that puts dust into the air. Wet them with disinfectant first and wipe them up. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

Cleanup steps from the CDC

Cleanup follows a fixed order. The CDC advises airing out the space for 30 minutes, wearing gloves, soaking droppings and nests with disinfectant, wiping them up with paper towels, double bagging the waste and washing hands afterward. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

How small a gap a rat needs

The entry that allowed the nest has to be closed. NC State Extension says young rats can pass through an opening of about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. That is smaller than most people expect when they look at a wall opening. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Why a dead rodent can end up in a wall

Animals in the nest should be removed before it is cleared. The EPA says anticoagulant baits take about 4 days to 2 weeks to kill. A poisoned rodent can die inside a wall, attic or crawl space, which is why trapping is preferred indoors. (Source: US EPA, rodenticides)

What mouse droppings tell you

For Rat Nest Removal: NC State Extension puts a mouse at roughly 50 to 75 droppings a day, each about a quarter to a half inch long. A small area can hold a lot of them, which is why a mouse problem is often found by droppings first. That volume is why even a small mouse problem leaves obvious evidence. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Rat Nest Removal: Questions People Ask

What does a rat nest look like?

A mass of shredded paper, insulation, fabric or plant material, usually in a hidden, protected spot such as an attic corner or wall void.

Should I clear a nest myself?

Only with protection. The CDC advises gloves, ventilation and wetting the nest with disinfectant before removing it, and says not to sweep or vacuum.

Do rats reuse nests?

They can. Removing the nest and sealing the entry point keeps new animals from using the site.

Where do Norway rats nest?

UC IPM says Norway rats burrow along foundations and under rubbish and woodpiles.
