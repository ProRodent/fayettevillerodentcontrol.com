About this service

What Rat Proofing Covers

Rats need less room than most people think. NC State Extension says young rats can pass through about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. Rat proofing finds those openings and closes them with materials a rat cannot gnaw.

It works best when done after the animals have been removed, or timed with removal. The work covers roofline and soffit gaps, vents, utility entries, foundation cracks and doors, and it adds outside steps such as cutting back limbs and moving woodpiles.

Signs

When to Call About Rat Proofing

  • Gnawed holes in soffits or siding
  • Rats seen on the roof or fence
  • Droppings by a vent or pipe
  • Daylight around a garage door
  • Damaged vent screens

A common mistake

Using chicken wire or foam is the usual error. Rats chew foam and push through chicken wire. Using the wrong material means paying twice.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Rat Proofing

How small a gap a rat needs

Size is the starting point for any rat proofing plan. NC State Extension says young rats can pass through an opening of about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. That is smaller than most people expect when they look at a wall opening. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Screening vents against rats

Vent screening has to be tighter than chicken wire. UC IPM recommends half inch mesh over vents and says chicken wire is not enough to stop rats. NC State's quarter inch hardware cloth is the tighter standard, and it also keeps mice out. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Cutting the route to the roof

Rats reach roofs along limbs and wires, so those are part of the job. UC IPM lists limb trimming, with a clearance range of 3 to 6 feet, as a way to cut off the route roof rats use to reach a roof. Trees and wires that touch the house act as bridges. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Door gaps and sweeps

Door gaps are one of the most overlooked rat entries. NC State Extension advises keeping the clearance under a door to no more than a quarter inch and mentions kick plates and rodent-proof door sweeps for garage and storage doors that take wear. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Where rats nest

Nest location points to the openings that matter. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Rat Proofing: Questions People Ask

What size gap can a rat use?

About a half inch for an adult rat and about a quarter inch for a young rat, according to NC State Extension.

Is chicken wire enough?

No. UC IPM says chicken wire will not stop rats and recommends half inch mesh over vents. NC State recommends quarter inch hardware cloth, which also stops mice.

Can foam close a rat hole?

Not on its own. NC State does not recommend foam as a stand-alone seal because rodents chew through it.

When should proofing be done?

After the rats are out of the building or timed with trapping, so animals are not sealed inside.
