Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Military Housing Rodent Control

How small a gap a mouse needs

Garage corners and door gaps are the first places mice get in. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

What mouse droppings tell you

Droppings are often the first sign during a move-in. NC State Extension puts a mouse at roughly 50 to 75 droppings a day, each about a quarter to a half inch long. A small area can hold a lot of them, which is why a mouse problem is often found by droppings first. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings

Do not sweep droppings in a newly vacant house. The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests, because that puts dust into the air. Wet them with disinfectant first and wipe them up. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

Checking traps daily

Trap checks continue after a move. NC State Extension advises checking traps daily and keeping mouse traps for mice and rat traps for rats. A trap that is too small for the animal can injure it without killing it and teach it to avoid the spot. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Cleanup steps from the CDC

Cleanup in a vacant house follows safe steps. The CDC advises airing out the space for 30 minutes, wearing gloves, soaking droppings and nests with disinfectant, wiping them up with paper towels, double bagging the waste and washing hands afterward. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

How small a gap a rat needs

For Military Housing Rodent Control: NC State Extension says young rats can pass through an opening of about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. That is smaller than most people expect when they look at a wall opening. Anything this size or larger needs a proper seal. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)
