How the work fits together

Rodent Control Is Four Jobs in One Order

Every rodent problem follows the same order: find out what you have, remove the animals, close the openings and clean up. Doing them out of order is how problems come back. Sealing a building with rodents inside traps them, and cleaning before the animals are gone only invites new contamination.

The services below are grouped by the job they do. Removal and inspection come first. Exclusion and prevention is the step that keeps the work from being repeated. Cleanup covers droppings, nests and insulation. Property-type pages explain how the same work changes for a house, a restaurant, a warehouse or a rental near Fort Bragg.

What the guidance says

Facts Behind the Way the Work Is Done

How small a gap a mouse needs

NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

How small a gap a rat needs

NC State Extension says young rats can pass through an opening of about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. That is smaller than most people expect when they look at a wall opening. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Where rats nest

UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

What the EPA allows consumers

The EPA restricts consumer rodenticides to ready-to-use bait stations and says second-generation anticoagulant products are not for consumer use. Anything stronger is for trained applicators. (Source: US EPA, rodenticides)

Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings

The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests, because that puts dust into the air. Wet them with disinfectant first and wipe them up. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

Common questions

Rodent Control Services: Common Questions

Do I need an inspection first?

Yes. Every job starts with an inspection to identify the species, the nesting place and the entry points, and the quote is written after that inspection.

Do you use poison?

Indoors, traps are used. The EPA restricts consumer rodenticides to ready-to-use bait stations and requires tamper-resistant stations where children, pets or wildlife could reach bait, so any bait is placed outdoors in such stations.

How do I choose between services?

Start with the inspection. Removal, sealing and cleanup are then recommended in order. If you are not sure which page fits, call and describe what you found.
