About this service

What Restaurant Rodent Control Covers

Restaurants combine food, water and warmth with frequent door traffic, which is what rodents look for. The risk areas are back doors, dry storage, dish areas, grease and trash pads and the gaps where pipes enter walls.

A restaurant visit looks at those areas first. It records activity, lists the gaps and sanitation points that need action, recommends sealing and trap placement that fits kitchen rules and gives management written notes. We do not claim any specific health inspection result.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Restaurant Rodent Control

Door gaps and sweeps

The back door is the first place to look. NC State Extension advises keeping the clearance under a door to no more than a quarter inch and mentions kick plates and rodent-proof door sweeps for garage and storage doors that take wear. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

How small a gap a mouse needs

A kitchen has many small gaps at pipes and equipment. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Where rat traps go

Outdoor rats need trap spacing built for rats. UC IPM says to space snap traps about 10 to 20 feet apart for rats and to set them off the ground, on beams or shelves, for roof rats, which travel along elevated routes. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

How small a gap a rat needs

Rats can pass a half inch gap at back doors. NC State Extension says young rats can pass through an opening of about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. That is smaller than most people expect when they look at a wall opening. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Rub marks and travel routes

Rub marks reveal routes behind equipment. NC State Extension lists rub marks along walls and pipes among the signs of rat travel. Those marks follow the same route night after night, which is where inspection and trapping effort pays off. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Cutting the route to the roof

For Restaurant Rodent Control: UC IPM lists limb trimming, with a clearance range of 3 to 6 feet, as a way to cut off the route roof rats use to reach a roof. Trees and wires that touch the house act as bridges. Clearance is a one-time job that keeps a route closed for years. (Source: UC IPM, rats)
