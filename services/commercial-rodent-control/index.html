About this service

What Commercial Rodent Control Covers

A commercial building has more entry routes than a house: dock doors, roll-up doors, shared walls, utility chases, rooftop equipment and dumpster areas. It is also open for deliveries and staff traffic, so rodents get chances a house does not give them.

A commercial job begins with a full inspection and a map of activity. The plan covers sealing, trapping, sanitation advice for storage and waste areas and a written record of findings that managers can keep. Work is scheduled around business hours.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Commercial Rodent Control

Door gaps and sweeps

Doors are the first line of defense in a business. NC State Extension advises keeping the clearance under a door to no more than a quarter inch and mentions kick plates and rodent-proof door sweeps for garage and storage doors that take wear. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Where mouse traps go

Spacing fits mice and rats. UC IPM says mice seldom travel more than about 30 feet from the nest, so traps are spaced no more than about 10 feet apart and set in pairs 1 to 2 inches apart along walls where mice run. (Source: UC IPM, house mouse)

Tamper-resistant stations

Outdoor stations around a business need extra care. The EPA requires tamper-resistant bait stations where children under six, pets or wildlife could reach the bait. A station that a child or a dog can open is not an acceptable placement. (Source: US EPA, rodenticide product restrictions)

How small a gap a mouse needs

Door and wall gaps are the first check at a business. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Prevention is the main control

Sanitation is part of the plan. The CDC states that controlling rodents in and around the home is the primary way to prevent rodent-borne disease such as hantavirus. Sealing and sanitation come before any trap or bait. (Source: CDC, rodent control)

Where rats nest

For Commercial Rodent Control: UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. Nest location tells you where to start looking. (Source: UC IPM, rats)
