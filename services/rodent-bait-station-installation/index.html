What Bait Station Installation Covers

Rodenticide is the part of rodent control with the most rules. Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Bait Station Installation

What the EPA allows consumers

Rules limit what can be used. The EPA restricts consumer rodenticides to ready-to-use bait stations and says second-generation anticoagulant products are not for consumer use. Anything stronger is for trained applicators. (Source: US EPA, rodenticides)

Tamper-resistant stations

Stations must keep bait away from children and pets. The EPA requires tamper-resistant bait stations where children under six, pets or wildlife could reach the bait. A station that a child or a dog can open is not an acceptable placement. (Source: US EPA, rodenticide product restrictions)

Why a dead rodent can end up in a wall

Delayed action is the reason indoor bait is avoided. The EPA says anticoagulant baits take about 4 days to 2 weeks to kill. A poisoned rodent can die inside a wall, attic or crawl space, which is why trapping is preferred indoors. (Source: US EPA, rodenticides)

Where rats nest

Burrow locations decide where stations go. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

How small a gap a rat needs

Stations do not replace sealing. NC State Extension says young rats can pass through an opening of about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. That is smaller than most people expect when they look at a wall opening. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Door gaps and sweeps

For Bait Station Installation: NC State Extension advises keeping the clearance under a door to no more than a quarter inch and mentions kick plates and rodent-proof door sweeps for garage and storage doors that take wear. A strip of daylight under a door is a gap that a rodent can use. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings

For Bait Station Installation: The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests, because that puts dust into the air. Wet them with disinfectant first and wipe them up. Dust is the hazard, so wetting first is the key step. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)
