About this service

What Rodent Droppings Cleanup Covers

Rodent droppings and urine can carry disease. The CDC states that controlling rodents in and around the home is the primary prevention for diseases such as hantavirus, and that cleanup must be done in a way that does not stir up dust.

Cleanup follows the CDC order: ventilate the area, wear gloves, spray droppings with disinfectant, let it soak, wipe up with paper towels, double bag the waste and disinfect surfaces. This service follows those steps in attics, garages, kitchens and crawl spaces.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Rodent Droppings Cleanup

Cleanup steps from the CDC

This is the cleanup order the CDC recommends. The CDC advises airing out the space for 30 minutes, wearing gloves, soaking droppings and nests with disinfectant, wiping them up with paper towels, double bagging the waste and washing hands afterward. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

The CDC bleach mix

A fresh bleach mix is part of the method. The CDC gives a disinfectant mix of 1.5 cups of household bleach per gallon of water, made fresh, left on the surface for 5 minutes before wiping. Household disinfectants are also acceptable. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

What mouse droppings tell you

A mouse problem leaves a lot of small droppings. NC State Extension puts a mouse at roughly 50 to 75 droppings a day, each about a quarter to a half inch long. A small area can hold a lot of them, which is why a mouse problem is often found by droppings first. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Checking traps daily

Removal comes before cleanup. NC State Extension advises checking traps daily and keeping mouse traps for mice and rat traps for rats. A trap that is too small for the animal can injure it without killing it and teach it to avoid the spot. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

How small a gap a mouse needs

Cleanup is followed by sealing the entry. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

How fast mice multiply

For Rodent Droppings Cleanup: UC IPM reports that a female house mouse can have 5 to 10 litters a year of about 5 to 6 pups, with gestation of 18 to 21 days and sexual maturity at 5 to 6 weeks. A few mice can become dozens in a season. Waiting a few weeks can multiply the number of animals to deal with. (Source: UC IPM, house mouse)

Rodent Droppings Cleanup: Questions People Ask

Can I sweep or vacuum droppings?

No. The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings, because it puts dust in the air. Wet them first with disinfectant.

What disinfectant should I use?

The CDC gives 1.5 cups of household bleach per gallon of water, mixed fresh and left for 5 minutes, or a household disinfectant.

How many droppings does a rodent leave?

NC State puts a mouse at 50 to 75 droppings a day and a rat at 30 to 50, so a small area can have a lot.

Should I clean before the rodents are gone?

No. Cleaning first only invites new contamination. Removal and sealing come first.
