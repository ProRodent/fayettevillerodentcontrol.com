About this service

What Urgent Rat Removal Covers

Rats in living areas carry droppings, urine and gnawing damage into places where food is stored and prepared. A single sighting often means a nest nearby, because rats are rarely alone. Quick action means identifying the route and putting traps on it.

For an urgent rat call, the first look is at where the animal was seen and the routes leading to it: pipes, wall edges, cabinets, the attic and the crawl space. Traps go along those routes at the spacing the guidance recommends, and sealing begins once activity has stopped.

Signs

When to Call About Urgent Rat Removal

  • A rat seen inside the house
  • Droppings along a wall or in a cabinet
  • Chewed wires or pipes
  • Rub marks on baseboards
  • Noises in the ceiling

A common mistake

Trapping only where the rat was seen is the usual error. Traps must go along the route, not at the sighting. Another is not removing food, so more rats arrive.

What Published Guidance Says About Urgent Rat Removal

What rat droppings tell you

Droppings show the route the rat takes at night. NC State Extension puts a rat at roughly 30 to 50 droppings a day, each about a half to three quarters of an inch long. Fresh, dark droppings in a spot mean the route is in use now. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Where rat traps go

Rat traps are spaced farther apart than mouse traps. UC IPM says to space snap traps about 10 to 20 feet apart for rats and to set them off the ground, on beams or shelves, for roof rats, which travel along elevated routes. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Checking traps daily

Daily checks keep the response safe and humane. NC State Extension advises checking traps daily and keeping mouse traps for mice and rat traps for rats. A trap that is too small for the animal can injure it without killing it and teach it to avoid the spot. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

How small a gap a rat needs

Rats need about a half inch. NC State Extension says young rats can pass through an opening of about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. That is smaller than most people expect when they look at a wall opening. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Where rats nest

Nest location shapes where traps go. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Common questions

Urgent Rat Removal: Questions People Ask

Does one rat sighting mean more?

Often. Rats are rarely alone, and a sighting in living areas suggests a nest nearby. NC State puts a rat at 30 to 50 droppings a day, so signs build quickly.

What kind of trap is used for a rat?

A rat-size snap trap. NC State advises not using mouse traps for rats or the reverse, and says to check traps daily.

Will the rat leave on its own?

Unlikely if food and shelter are still available. Removal and sealing are needed.

Is it safe to touch droppings?

Not with bare hands. The CDC advises gloves, disinfectant and wet cleanup, and says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
