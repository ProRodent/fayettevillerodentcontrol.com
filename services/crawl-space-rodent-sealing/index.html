About this service

What Crawl Space Rodent Sealing Covers

A crawl space is protected by vents, an access door and a foundation wall, and each can have gaps. Rats and mice use them to reach pipes, ducts and insulation, and from there often move into wall voids and the floor above.

Sealing starts with a look at the vents, the access hatch and the outside of the foundation, because Norway rats burrow along foundations. Damaged vent screens are replaced with hardware cloth, gaps are closed with metal or concrete and the area is cleaned after the animals are gone.

Signs

When to Call About Crawl Space Rodent Sealing

  • Droppings or gnawing on insulation or ductwork
  • Torn or missing vent screens
  • Burrows beside the foundation
  • Odors rising from the floor
  • Noises under the floor

A common mistake

Closing the vents completely is a mistake. Vents are needed for airflow, so they are screened with hardware cloth instead of being blocked. Another is sealing before trapping, which traps animals under the house.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Crawl Space Rodent Sealing

Vents need metal, not filler

Vent screens are the main thing sealed in a crawl space. NC State Extension recommends quarter inch hardware cloth over vents and does not recommend expanding foam as a stand-alone seal. Rodents chew soft materials, so metal mesh is what keeps an opening closed. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Where rats nest

Norway rats burrow along the very walls that hold the crawl space. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Storms and flooding push rodents indoors

Water and storms move rodents toward crawl spaces. NC State Extension notes that flooding pushes rodents into attics and that Norway rats are the most common rat in North Carolina. After a storm, new gaps and wet debris are both worth checking. (Source: NC State Extension, pest problems after storms: rodents)

How small a gap a rat needs

Foundation gaps are measured against rat sizes. NC State Extension says young rats can pass through an opening of about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. That is smaller than most people expect when they look at a wall opening. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings

Dry droppings in a crawl space should not be disturbed. The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests, because that puts dust into the air. Wet them with disinfectant first and wipe them up. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

Crawl Space Rodent Sealing: Questions People Ask

Why do rodents get into crawl spaces?

Vents, access doors and foundation gaps give them a way in, and the space offers shelter, pipes to follow and insulation to nest in.

What should vent screens be made of?

NC State recommends quarter inch hardware cloth. UC IPM recommends half inch mesh for rats and says chicken wire is not enough.

Does flooding matter?

NC State notes that flooding pushes rodents into attics and other building spaces, so crawl spaces should be checked for new gaps after high water.

Should insulation be replaced?

If rodents have nested in or soiled it, replacement may be needed after cleanup. See the insulation replacement page for how that is handled.
