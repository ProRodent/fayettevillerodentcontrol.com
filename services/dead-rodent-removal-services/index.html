About this service

What Dead Rodent Removal Covers

Dead rodents are most often found in places people cannot see: wall voids, attics, crawl spaces and under floors. The signs are a persistent odor, flies and sometimes staining. The odor usually fades over days to weeks, but the carcass is still a sanitation problem until it is removed.

Removal starts with finding the source, which can mean opening a section of wall, ceiling or access panel. The carcass is double bagged and removed, the area is cleaned with disinfectant and the entry point that let the animal in is identified.

When to Call About Dead Rodent Removal

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Dead Rodent Removal

Why a dead rodent can end up in a wall

Poison is why many dead rodents end up inside walls. The EPA says anticoagulant baits take about 4 days to 2 weeks to kill. A poisoned rodent can die inside a wall, attic or crawl space, which is why trapping is preferred indoors. (Source: US EPA, rodenticides)

Cleanup steps from the CDC

Safe handling steps matter as much as finding the animal. The CDC advises airing out the space for 30 minutes, wearing gloves, soaking droppings and nests with disinfectant, wiping them up with paper towels, double bagging the waste and washing hands afterward. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

The CDC bleach mix

The disinfectant mix is simple and specific. The CDC gives a disinfectant mix of 1.5 cups of household bleach per gallon of water, made fresh, left on the surface for 5 minutes before wiping. Household disinfectants are also acceptable. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

Prevention is the main control

Safe removal protects the household. The CDC states that controlling rodents in and around the home is the primary way to prevent rodent-borne disease such as hantavirus. Sealing and sanitation come before any trap or bait. (Source: CDC, rodent control)

Checking traps daily

Daily trap checks reduce the chance of an animal dying out of reach. NC State Extension advises checking traps daily and keeping mouse traps for mice and rat traps for rats. A trap that is too small for the animal can injure it without killing it and teach it to avoid the spot. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)
