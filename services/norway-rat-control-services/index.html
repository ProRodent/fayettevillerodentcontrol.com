About this service

What Norway Rat Control Covers

The Norway rat is the most common rat that invades structures in North Carolina, according to NC State Extension. It is a ground animal. It digs burrows along foundations, under slabs, woodpiles and debris and uses them as a base to reach food and shelter.

Norway rat control therefore starts at ground level. The inspection looks for burrows, runways and gnaw marks outside, then follows the evidence in through crawl space vents, pipe gaps and foundation cracks. Trapping and sealing follow, along with removal of whatever feeds the colony.

Signs

When to Call About Norway Rat Control

  • Burrow holes along the foundation, fence line or under a shed
  • Droppings near trash, pet food or compost
  • Gnawing at the base of walls or doors
  • Smears and rub marks along runways
  • Rats seen at ground level at dusk

A common mistake

Filling a burrow with dirt without sealing the building is the usual mistake. The rats dig a new entrance. Leaving garbage cans open and pet food outside also keeps the colony fed.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Norway Rat Control

Storms and flooding push rodents indoors

Norway rats are the common rat in this state, and storms change where they go. NC State Extension notes that flooding pushes rodents into attics and that Norway rats are the most common rat in North Carolina. After a storm, new gaps and wet debris are both worth checking. (Source: NC State Extension, pest problems after storms: rodents)

Baiting a burrow

Burrow baiting follows strict rules. The EPA allows outdoor burrow baiting only with pelleted bait placed at least 6 inches down the burrow. Bait left on top of the ground is a hazard to pets and wildlife. (Source: US EPA, rodenticide product restrictions)

How small a gap a rat needs

Adult rats need about a half inch to pass. NC State Extension says young rats can pass through an opening of about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. That is smaller than most people expect when they look at a wall opening. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Checking traps daily

Daily trap checks are part of Norway rat work. NC State Extension advises checking traps daily and keeping mouse traps for mice and rat traps for rats. A trap that is too small for the animal can injure it without killing it and teach it to avoid the spot. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Storm debris is shelter

Debris near the foundation shelters burrows. NC State Extension says debris left by a storm gives rodents shelter near a home. Stacked limbs, damaged fencing and soaked stored items near the foundation should be moved or removed. (Source: NC State Extension, pest problems after storms: rodents)

Norway Rat Control: Questions People Ask

Where do Norway rats nest?

UC IPM says Norway rats burrow along foundations and under rubbish and woodpiles. Roof rats are the ones that nest above ground in attics and wall voids.

Is burying bait in a burrow allowed?

The EPA allows outdoor burrow baiting only with pelleted bait placed at least 6 inches down, and tamper-resistant stations are required where children, pets or wildlife could reach the bait.

Do storms bring more Norway rats?

NC State Extension notes that flooding pushes rodents into attics and that storm debris gives them shelter. It is worth checking for new gaps and debris after high water.

How do I keep them out?

Seal gaps with metal and mesh, keep stored items off the ground and away from the foundation, and secure garbage and pet food.
