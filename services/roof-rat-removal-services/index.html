About this service

What Roof Rat Removal Covers

Roof rats are climbers. They reach a house along tree limbs, wires and fences, nest in attics and wall voids, and move along beams and pipes. Scratching or running sounds overhead at night are a common first sign.

Roof rat work therefore concentrates above the ground floor: the roofline, soffits, vents and attic. Traps are set off the ground along the routes the animals use, entry points at the roof edge are sealed, and the limbs or wires that act as bridges are pointed out.

Signs

When to Call About Roof Rat Removal

  • Scratching or running overhead at dusk or night
  • Droppings in the attic or on top of walls
  • Gnawed vent screens or soffits
  • Rub marks on beams and pipes
  • Rats seen on wires, fences or limbs near the house

A common mistake

Setting traps on the ground floor is the most common error. Roof rats work from above, so traps belong on beams and along elevated routes. Leaving a limb touching the roof also keeps the route open after removal.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Roof Rat Removal

Where rats nest

Roof rats nest high, so the attic is where the plan begins. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Where rat traps go

Traps for roof rats go off the ground. UC IPM says to space snap traps about 10 to 20 feet apart for rats and to set them off the ground, on beams or shelves, for roof rats, which travel along elevated routes. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Cutting the route to the roof

Trees touching the roof give roof rats an easy route. UC IPM lists limb trimming, with a clearance range of 3 to 6 feet, as a way to cut off the route roof rats use to reach a roof. Trees and wires that touch the house act as bridges. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

How small a gap a rat needs

Roofline gaps are measured against rat sizes. NC State Extension says young rats can pass through an opening of about a quarter inch and adult rats through about a half inch. That is smaller than most people expect when they look at a wall opening. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Storms and flooding push rodents indoors

Storm damage can open new gaps at the roof edge. NC State Extension notes that flooding pushes rodents into attics and that Norway rats are the most common rat in North Carolina. After a storm, new gaps and wet debris are both worth checking. (Source: NC State Extension, pest problems after storms: rodents)

Common questions

Roof Rat Removal: Questions People Ask

How do I know it is a roof rat?

UC IPM lists roof rats as 5 to 10 ounces and Norway rats as 7 to 18 ounces. Roof rats nest above ground, so evidence in the attic and overhead noises point to them.

Where should traps go for roof rats?

Off the ground, on beams, shelves and along elevated runways. UC IPM says to space snap traps about 10 to 20 feet apart.

Do tree limbs matter?

Yes. Limbs and wires that touch the roof are routes in. UC IPM lists limb trimming, with a 3 to 6 foot clearance range, as a way to cut that route off.

What do roof rats do to an attic?

They nest in insulation, gnaw wiring and wood, and leave droppings. Cleanup and any insulation replacement come after the animals are gone.
