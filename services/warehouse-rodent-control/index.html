About this service

What Warehouse Rodent Control Covers

Warehouses have large footprints, many doors and long periods when parts of the building are untouched. Rodents use dock gaps, roll-up door seals and pallet stacks as cover and nesting sites, and they travel along walls.

A warehouse job starts with a perimeter walk and a door-by-door check, then covers wall lines, pallet storage and exterior ground. Trap lines follow walls at the spacing the guidance recommends, and perimeter sealing and housekeeping steps are listed.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Warehouse Rodent Control

Where rat traps go

Long wall lines need properly spaced rat traps. UC IPM says to space snap traps about 10 to 20 feet apart for rats and to set them off the ground, on beams or shelves, for roof rats, which travel along elevated routes. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Where mouse traps go

Mouse lines are spaced much closer. UC IPM says mice seldom travel more than about 30 feet from the nest, so traps are spaced no more than about 10 feet apart and set in pairs 1 to 2 inches apart along walls where mice run. (Source: UC IPM, house mouse)

Baiting a burrow

Outdoor burrow work has specific rules. The EPA allows outdoor burrow baiting only with pelleted bait placed at least 6 inches down the burrow. Bait left on top of the ground is a hazard to pets and wildlife. (Source: US EPA, rodenticide product restrictions)

How small a gap a mouse needs

Gaps at dock levelers allow mice. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

What rat droppings tell you

Droppings along wall lines show rat routes. NC State Extension puts a rat at roughly 30 to 50 droppings a day, each about a half to three quarters of an inch long. Fresh, dark droppings in a spot mean the route is in use now. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings

For Warehouse Rodent Control: The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests, because that puts dust into the air. Wet them with disinfectant first and wipe them up. Dust is the hazard, so wetting first is the key step. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

The CDC bleach mix

For Warehouse Rodent Control: The CDC gives a disinfectant mix of 1.5 cups of household bleach per gallon of water, made fresh, left on the surface for 5 minutes before wiping. Household disinfectants are also acceptable. Mixing fresh keeps the solution effective. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

Warehouse Rodent Control: Questions People Ask

How far apart are traps set?

UC IPM says to space mouse traps up to about 10 feet apart and rat snap traps about 10 to 20 feet apart.

Is bait used?

Outdoor tamper-resistant stations only, per EPA rules. Indoors, traps are used.

What do you check at docks?

Seals, thresholds and gaps around the leveler where mice and rats can pass.

How do pallets matter?

Stacks give cover and nesting space, so keeping them off the wall and off the floor helps inspection and trapping.
