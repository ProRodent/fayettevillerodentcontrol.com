What Published Guidance Says About PCS Rodent Inspection

What mouse droppings tell you

Droppings help judge how long the animals have been there. NC State Extension puts a mouse at roughly 50 to 75 droppings a day, each about a quarter to a half inch long. A small area can hold a lot of them, which is why a mouse problem is often found by droppings first. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

How small a gap a mouse needs

Gaps at doors are easy to document. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Rub marks and travel routes

Rub marks show routes in a vacant house. NC State Extension lists rub marks along walls and pipes among the signs of rat travel. Those marks follow the same route night after night, which is where inspection and trapping effort pays off. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading rats)

Checking traps daily

Trap checks follow an inspection that finds activity. NC State Extension advises checking traps daily and keeping mouse traps for mice and rat traps for rats. A trap that is too small for the animal can injure it without killing it and teach it to avoid the spot. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Cleanup steps from the CDC

Cleanup in a vacant house follows safe steps. The CDC advises airing out the space for 30 minutes, wearing gloves, soaking droppings and nests with disinfectant, wiping them up with paper towels, double bagging the waste and washing hands afterward. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

Tamper-resistant stations

For PCS Rodent Inspection: The EPA requires tamper-resistant bait stations where children under six, pets or wildlife could reach the bait. A station that a child or a dog can open is not an acceptable placement. Placement matters as much as the product. (Source: US EPA, rodenticide product restrictions)

Storms and flooding push rodents indoors

For PCS Rodent Inspection: NC State Extension notes that flooding pushes rodents into attics and that Norway rats are the most common rat in North Carolina. After a storm, new gaps and wet debris are both worth checking. A quick walk around the property after a storm is time well spent. (Source: NC State Extension, pest problems after storms: rodents)
