About this service

What Urgent Rodent Removal Covers

Some rodent problems cannot wait: a rat seen in a kitchen, mice in a child's room, scratching in a wall every night or droppings in food storage. These calls are treated as urgent. The first goal is to get a clear picture of what is happening and to stop the contamination.

An urgent call still follows the same sequence. Identify the species, find the activity and entry points, start trapping and sealing and clean up with safe handling. What changes is the order: the areas where people live, cook and sleep come first.

Research-based guidance

What Published Guidance Says About Urgent Rodent Removal

Prevention is the main control

Health risk is the reason urgent calls come first. The CDC states that controlling rodents in and around the home is the primary way to prevent rodent-borne disease such as hantavirus. Sealing and sanitation come before any trap or bait. (Source: CDC, rodent control)

Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings

Cleaning dry droppings the wrong way spreads the risk. The CDC says not to sweep or vacuum dry droppings or nests, because that puts dust into the air. Wet them with disinfectant first and wipe them up. (Source: CDC, rodent cleanup)

What mouse droppings tell you

Droppings accumulate quickly in living areas. NC State Extension puts a mouse at roughly 50 to 75 droppings a day, each about a quarter to a half inch long. A small area can hold a lot of them, which is why a mouse problem is often found by droppings first. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Where rat traps go

Quick trap setup depends on the right trap for the species. UC IPM says to space snap traps about 10 to 20 feet apart for rats and to set them off the ground, on beams or shelves, for roof rats, which travel along elevated routes. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

Checking traps daily

Daily checks are part of an urgent job. NC State Extension advises checking traps daily and keeping mouse traps for mice and rat traps for rats. A trap that is too small for the animal can injure it without killing it and teach it to avoid the spot. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Common questions

Urgent Rodent Removal: Questions People Ask

What counts as urgent?

A rodent seen in living areas, droppings in food storage, noises in walls at night or a dead animal with odor. Any of these is reason to call.

What should I do before anyone arrives?

Store food in sealed containers, keep pets and children away from droppings and do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings. The CDC says to wet them with disinfectant first.

Is poison used in an urgent case?

Not indoors. The EPA notes that poisoned rodents take days to die and may die in walls, so trapping is the indoor method.

Is it safe to clean droppings myself?

With care, yes. The CDC advises airing out the space, wearing gloves, soaking with disinfectant and double bagging the waste.
