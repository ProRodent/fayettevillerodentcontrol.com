Research-based

What the Published Guidance Says

How small a gap a mouse needs

Everything starts with measuring the gaps. NC State Extension reports that an adult mouse can get through an opening of about a quarter inch. A gap that size is easy to miss at a door corner, a pipe collar or a vent edge. (Source: NC State Extension, common structure-invading mice)

Where rats nest

Where the animal nests decides where the work starts. UC IPM explains that roof rats nest above ground, in attics and wall voids, while Norway rats burrow along foundations and under debris. The nesting place decides where traps, sealing and cleanup start. (Source: UC IPM, rats)

What the EPA allows consumers

Poison has legal limits, so traps and sealing come first. The EPA restricts consumer rodenticides to ready-to-use bait stations and says second-generation anticoagulant products are not for consumer use. Anything stronger is for trained applicators. (Source: US EPA, rodenticides)

Species identification

Norway Rat, Roof Rat or House Mouse?

The plan depends on the species, so identification comes first. Droppings, size and nest location are the quickest clues.

Common questions

Rodent Control Questions

Do you offer rodent control in Fayetteville?

Yes. Call (844) 635-0403 for rat and mouse control in Fayetteville, the rest of Cumberland County and nearby towns within roughly a 45 minute drive.

Do you use poison?

Indoors the work uses traps. The EPA limits consumer rodenticides to ready-to-use bait stations and requires tamper-resistant stations where children, pets or wildlife could reach bait, so any bait is placed outdoors in such stations.

How do I tell rats from mice?

Size and droppings are the main clues. NC State puts rat droppings at about a half to three quarters of an inch and mouse droppings at about a quarter to a half inch.

How small a gap can rodents use?

NC State reports that mice can use a gap of about a quarter inch, young rats about a quarter inch and adult rats about a half inch.
